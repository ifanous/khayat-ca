# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Three visitor types with equal strategic weight, but the site is designed
**skeptic-first** — if it earns the most skeptical visitor's trust, it earns
everyone's.

1. **The researcher.** A prospective immigrant comparing 3–4 RCICs late at
   night. They are anxious, time-pressed, and will cross-check credentials,
   reviews, and tone before booking anything. They need to see a real licensed
   human, not a firm.
2. **The sponsor.** A family member already in Canada trying to bring a spouse,
   parent, or child over. Emotionally loaded. They need reassurance that the
   process is survivable and that their case will be handled precisely.
3. **The referral.** Someone told them "talk to Brigitte." They just need to
   confirm she's legit and find the contact form quickly.

All three share one job-to-be-done: *decide whether to trust Brigitte with a
life-changing, expensive, high-stakes decision.*

## Product Purpose

A marketing site that establishes Brigitte Khayat as the name that appears — and
the name that is trusted — when someone in the GTA searches for an immigration
consultant.

**Primary outcome:** organic search authority for GTA immigration queries
(RCIC-IRB, refugee claims, sponsorship, PR, work/study permits).

**Secondary outcome:** qualified consultation bookings. "Qualified" matters.
Brigitte will not take on cases she believes are unwinnable; the site's job is
to attract people she can actually help, not to maximize intake volume.

Success looks like: visitors who arrive from search finish reading, trust what
they read, and either book a consultation or leave knowing Brigitte's name for
later. It does **not** look like dashboard-style conversion-at-all-costs.

## Brand Personality

Three words: **honest, grounded, rigorous.**

Two modes held in tension:

- **Warm & human.** Brigitte is a person, not a firm. First-person voice ("I"),
  not the faceless consultancy "we." Calm, unhurried, the way a senior clinician
  or a trusted family advisor speaks. Multilingual by lived experience (EN, FR,
  AR, EL) — this is a fact about Brigitte, not a marketing boast.
- **Precise & rigorous.** No BS. No embellishment. If a case is weak, Brigitte
  says so; the site should carry that same integrity. Credentials (RCIC-IRB,
  CICC, CAPIC, IRB authorization) do real load-bearing work — they are not
  decorations.

Tone rules:

- Straight-talking. Short sentences. Specific claims over general adjectives.
- Never manufacture urgency. Immigration is already stressful.
- Never imply outcomes Brigitte can't control.
- Say "I" when Brigitte is speaking. Say what the site is, not what it promises.

## Anti-references

**Clickbait consultant marketing.** Any site whose primary move is to bait
visitors into a "free consultation" with dream-selling copy ("your Canadian
journey starts here," "make your Canadian dream come true," "we guarantee…").
The explicit enemy.

**Manufactured urgency and scarcity.** Fake countdowns, "limited spots," "act
now before the rules change." Never.

**Stock immigration imagery.** Smiling diverse family at an airport arrivals
gate. Maple leaves. Red-and-white flag flourishes. Passport-in-hand hero shots.
Skyline-of-Toronto-at-sunset hero shots. All banned.

**Testimonial walls that feel coached.** Rows of identical 5-star cards with
generic quotes. If reviews appear, they appear as real Google reviews with
real sourcing, not as marketing collateral.

**The two category reflexes to avoid.**

1. *Immigration-category reflex:* red + white + Canadian flag + navy +
   "pathway" language.
2. *Professional-services reflex:* law-firm navy-and-gold, heavy serifs, marble
   lobby vibe. Brigitte is not a white-shoe firm.

Neither is the right register. The target is closer to a trusted senior
practitioner's site — grounded, spacious, text-forward, credential-first.

## Design Principles

1. **Honesty is the aesthetic.** Every claim on the site must survive a "would
   Brigitte say this to a client's face?" test. If a headline feels
   aspirational or hypey, rewrite it until it reads like something she would
   actually say. Specificity beats adjectives.
2. **Credentials do the selling, not copy.** RCIC-IRB license, CICC, CAPIC,
   IRB representation authority, real Google reviews. Make these legible and
   structurally important — not logo-strip decoration. A skeptic should be able
   to verify Brigitte in under 30 seconds.
3. **Skeptic-first.** Design for the 2am comparison shopper. Remove anything
   that feels like marketing theatre (urgency, vague superlatives, stock
   warmth). What remains earns the other two audiences for free.
4. **One real person, not a brand.** Singular voice, singular face. Brigitte's
   name, presence, and voice are the product. Avoid the faceless "we,"
   corporate mascot illustrations, and brand-system abstraction that hides the
   practitioner.
5. **Calm over conversion.** The site reduces anxiety; it never manufactures
   it. Spacious layouts, unhurried pacing, plain language. A relaxed reader who
   trusts the author converts better than an agitated one who was nudged.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **Target:** WCAG 2.2 AA minimum, push toward AAA where practical (contrast,
  target size, focus visibility) — honest commitment, not aspirational.
- **Motion:** `prefers-reduced-motion` fully respected. No motion is ever
  load-bearing for comprehension.
- **Keyboard and screen reader:** all interactive paths (nav, theme toggle,
  contact form, blog) keyboard-reachable with visible focus and meaningful
  labels.
- **Touch targets:** 44×44 minimum.
- **Language:** English only for now. FR/AR/EL are spoken by Brigitte in
  consultations — the site surfaces this as a credential, not as a site-wide
  localization promise.
- **Cognitive load:** readers arriving in crisis (refugee, urgent family
  matters) must be able to find contact info without reading the whole page.
