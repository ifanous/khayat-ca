The Government of Canada has extended the Francophone Minority Communities Student Pilot, or FMCSP, until August 2027. The pilot gives eligible French-speaking international students a possible pathway to permanent residence after they complete their studies in Canada.

The pilot was originally scheduled to end on August 25, 2026. It is intended to attract French-speaking students to communities outside Quebec and encourage them to build their future in Canada.

A job offer is not required for the permanent residence pathway. Applicants must demonstrate French-language proficiency of at least NCLC 5 in speaking, listening, reading, and writing.

## Who can apply?

The FMCSP begins with an application for an FMCSP-specific study permit. Applicants generally must:

- Be a citizen of an eligible country and apply while outside Canada.
- Have a letter of acceptance from a participating designated learning institution outside Quebec confirming that they are applying under the FMCSP.
- Enrol in a full-time post-secondary program lasting at least two years.
- Complete a diploma or degree program in which more than 50% of instruction is delivered in French.
- Demonstrate French proficiency of at least NCLC 5 in all four language abilities.
- Show that they have enough money to pay tuition and cover living expenses for themselves and any accompanying family members.

Depending on their circumstances, applicants may also need to complete a medical examination or provide a police certificate.

## Eligible countries

The FMCSP is currently available to citizens of these countries:

Benin, Burkina Faso, Burundi, Cabo Verde, Cameroon, Central African Republic, Chad, Comoros, Côte d’Ivoire, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Dominica, Republic of the Congo, Djibouti, Egypt, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Guinea, Guinea-Bissau, Haiti, Lebanon, Madagascar, Mali, Mauritania, Mauritius, Morocco, Niger, Rwanda, Saint Lucia, São Tomé and Principe, Senegal, Seychelles, Togo, and Tunisia.

Citizenship alone does not guarantee eligibility. Applicants must meet all FMCSP requirements.

## Can family members accompany the student?

A spouse or common-law partner and dependent children may be able to accompany the applicant. Depending on their circumstances, family members may qualify for a visitor visa, study permit, or open work permit.

## From a study permit to permanent residence

The FMCSP has two stages.

### 1. Study in Canada

The applicant obtains an FMCSP-specific study permit and completes an eligible program at a participating institution outside Quebec.

### 2. Apply for permanent residence

After completing the program, the graduate may be eligible to apply for permanent residence under the FMCSP if they meet the applicable requirements. These include:

- Residing in Canada outside Quebec.
- Having valid temporary resident status.
- Completing an eligible diploma or degree while studying in Canada under the FMCSP.

Graduates who have completed their studies and submitted a permanent residence application may also qualify for an FMCSP-specific work permit while their application is processed.

## Participating institutions

The FMCSP includes participating institutions in several provinces, including Ontario, New Brunswick, Manitoba, Alberta, Saskatchewan, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island, and British Columbia.

Participating institutions include Collège Boréal, Collège La Cité, Université de Hearst, Université de l’Ontario, University of Ottawa, York University (Glendon), Université de Moncton, Université Saint-Boniface, University of Alberta, and University of Regina, among others.

Students should confirm that both their institution and their specific program are eligible under the FMCSP before applying.

## What applicants should know

The FMCSP may suit French-speaking students who want to study in Canada and pursue permanent residence afterward. The program has limited capacity, and the Government of Canada has not yet announced the study permit application cap for the August 2026 to August 2027 period.

The extension supports Canada's goal of increasing the number of French-speaking permanent residents settling outside Quebec, with a target of 12% by 2029.

For some students, the FMCSP provides an alternative to the more traditional route of obtaining a study permit, applying for a post-graduation work permit, and then applying through an economic immigration program.

Anyone considering the FMCSP should review their citizenship, French-language ability, intended program, educational institution, and overall immigration profile before making study plans.

Immigration programs and eligibility requirements can change. Applicants should consult the current IRCC requirements before submitting an application.
